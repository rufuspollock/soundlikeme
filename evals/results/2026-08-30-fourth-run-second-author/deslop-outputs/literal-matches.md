The seam was worked from above until 1913, when the company sank a second shaft and began to delve along the lower bed. Miners went down at six and did not come up until the shift ended; the only light was what they carried.

The museum's best object is not the winding gear but a tapestry, nine feet by four, worked by the wives of the men who died in the 1907 fall. It hangs in the second room. Each of the forty-one figures is stitched in a different colour of wool, and the border is a list of names.

The economics of the pit were never good. The owners had leverage over the leaseholder only for as long as the coal was cheap to lift, and once the lower bed flooded that leverage disappeared. By 1931 the leaseholder was in receivership.

The mortality estimates in the county records are more robust than the national series for the same period, because the parish clerk recorded cause of death and the national return did not. Vital statistics of this quality are rare before 1850.
