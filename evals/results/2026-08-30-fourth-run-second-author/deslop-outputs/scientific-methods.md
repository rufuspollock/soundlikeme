Samples were collected at 14-day intervals from six sites along the transect between March and September 2023. At each site, three replicate cores were taken to a depth of 15 cm and homogenised in the field. Cores were stored at 4 degrees C and processed within 72 hours of collection.

Dissolved organic carbon was measured by high-temperature catalytic oxidation. Blanks were run every tenth sample and the instrument was recalibrated whenever blank drift exceeded 5% of the running mean. Two samples from site D were excluded because the seal on the storage vial had failed; this exclusion is noted in the supplementary data.

The weighted interval score was used to evaluate the probabilistic forecasts, as it is proper and decomposes into calibration and sharpness components. Coverage was assessed at the 50% and 90% nominal levels.

Concentrations increased at all six sites over the sampling period, though the increase at site B may reflect the disturbance associated with the adjacent construction works rather than a seasonal trend. We cannot distinguish these explanations with the present design. The magnitude of the effect at the remaining five sites is consistent with previously reported seasonal ranges for catchments of this size, but the sample is small and the confidence intervals are correspondingly wide.

These results should be interpreted with caution. The transect covers a single catchment, and no attempt was made to control for land use upstream of site A.
