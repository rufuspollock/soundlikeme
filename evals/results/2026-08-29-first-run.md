## Read the 1.00s as noise

Four dimensions have a ceiling-floor gap of exactly one point. On those, "headroom closed = 1.00" means the candidate scored one point above the floor on a scale where one point is the entire measurable range. That is a single judge's rounding, not a result.

Only three dimensions discriminated with a gap of 2 — argument shape, signature moves, tells — and the candidate closed half the gap on each. The honest summary of this run is: **the profile closes about half the gap on the dimensions that can measure anything, and the other four dimensions did not resolve.**

The total (0.70) is arithmetically correct and misleading for the same reason. It is reported because the template asks for it, not because it means much.

## What the judges quoted

- **Stance (candidate, 5):** "It is wrong. Not wrong as a matter of emphasis or framing, but simply wrong" — commits where the reference commits, hedges where it hedges ("Whether that is actually true is unclear to me").
- **Rhythm (candidate, 4):** the candidate "smooths several of the reference's dash-and-parenthesis pile-ups into cleaner clauses", making the prose "a shade more polished and evenly paced than the original's slightly rougher, aside-heavy flow."
- **Signature moves (candidate, 4):** reproduces the apple/all-apples analogy, the footnote asides, the blunt one-line restatement and the Macaulay block quote, but trades the reference's inline parentheticals for em dashes.
- **Argument shape (floor, 3):** "the reference's characteristic moves are gone: it opens by stating the equation flatly rather than with a scene-setting lead... and it ends on a colophon plus footnotes rather than a rhetorical clincher."
- **Rhythm (floor, 3):** the reference's long sprawling sentences "have been broken into short declaratives, so the sprawl is lost."
- **Tells (floor, 3):** repeated binary contrast — "That is not an analogy to monopoly. It is the definition of monopoly." — plus a closing aphorism and a final one-line clincher.

## The most useful finding

The candidate's Rhythm and Signature-moves scores were both docked for the same thing, in the judge's own words: it is **more polished and more evenly paced than Pollock actually is.** The profile lists parenthetical asides and high sentence-length variance as markers, and the draft still came out tidier than the man.

This is the exact failure mode `protections.md` calls "flat is a tell too", appearing in the skill's own output. Fixing it is not a matter of adding a rule — the rule is already written and was already loaded. It is a matter of the markers being descriptive rather than binding. Worth trying: give `draft` an explicit instruction to overshoot the profile's roughness, on the theory that regression toward clean prose is the constant force and the draft should be aimed past the target.

## Harness bugs found

The first run was expected to debug the harness rather than the skill. It did.

1. **Judges guess the spelling convention instead of reading it.** Judge x cited "same British spelling" as evidence on a text using *emphasize*, *behaviors*, *recognizes*. Judge z credited the floor for "British spelling kept ('defence')" as a point of closeness — but the reference is American (*recognized*, *incentivizing*), so the floor was **rewarded for a spelling error**. Fix: the Lexicon anchor must tell the judge to determine the convention from the reference text and quote the evidence, not to assess it by impression.

2. **A hard anchor was ignored.** The rubric says invented specifics score Concreteness 1. The floor invented two — Helprin's first name ("Mark", not in the brief) and "revenue seventy years after death" (no term length is in the brief). Judge z spotted the second one, wrote it down, and scored 4 anyway. Fix: make the invented-specifics rule a gate stated before the anchors, the way the Tells gate is.

3. **The ceiling piece is badly matched.** *The Knowledge Commons is Different* is 501 words against the reference's 969, and it is a reading-note responding to a quotation rather than a sustained argument. It still scored 34/35, so it worked as a ceiling, but its Rhythm 4 caps that dimension at a gap of one and is probably an artifact of length. A closer ceiling would widen several gaps and make the measurement mean more.

4. **Three of seven dimensions carry the signal.** Not a bug exactly, but the eval as run has about three usable dimensions. Either the anchors need to spread scores further, or a single case is simply too little data — a second case would tell us which.
