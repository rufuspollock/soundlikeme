# Being a Noob

January 2020

When I was young, I assumed that older people had essentially everything figured out. Now that I am older myself, I can report that this assumption was mistaken.

In fact, I find that I feel like a noob almost constantly. I am generally either talking with a startup operating in a field I know nothing about, or working through a book on a subject I do not understand adequately, or traveling in a new country where I have little idea how anything actually works.

## The Paradox of Feeling Like a Noob

It is not an especially pleasant sensation, and "noob" is certainly not intended as a compliment. Yet it recently occurred to me that there is something genuinely encouraging concealed within the experience: the more of a noob you are locally, the less of a noob you are globally.

Consider a straightforward example. If you remain in your home country, you will tend to feel less like a noob than if you relocate to Farawavia, where everything operates according to unfamiliar rules. And yet you will almost certainly know more if you make the move. The feeling of being a noob, in other words, appears to be inversely correlated with actual ignorance.

This raises an important question. If the feeling of being a noob is in fact good for us, why do we find it so disagreeable? What evolutionary purpose could such an aversion plausibly serve?

## Why the Aversion Exists

My own view is that there are two distinct sources of the feeling: being stupid, and doing something novel. Our dislike of feeling like a noob is essentially our brain urging us onward, saying "Come on, come on, figure this out." For most of human history, this was arguably the correct thing to be thinking. The life of hunter-gatherers was undeniably complex, but it did not change nearly as rapidly as life changes today. They were not suddenly required to determine what to do about cryptocurrency. It therefore made a great deal of sense to be biased toward competence at existing problems rather than toward the discovery of new ones. It made sense for humans to dislike the feeling of being a noob, much as, in a world where food was scarce, it made sense for them to dislike the feeling of being hungry.

Now, however, too much food has become a more pressing problem than too little, and our dislike of feeling hungry consequently leads us astray. I would suggest that our dislike of feeling like a noob does precisely the same thing.

## Conclusion

The lesson, then, is worth stating plainly. Although the sensation is unpleasant, although it is unflattering, and although people will occasionally ridicule you for it, the more you feel like a noob, the better off you are likely to be.
