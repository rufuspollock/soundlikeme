# Being a Noob

January 2020

When I was young, I thought old people had everything figured out. Now that I'm old myself, I can report that they don't.

In fact, I feel like a noob almost constantly. I'm usually either talking to a startup in a field I know nothing about, or working through a book on a subject I don't understand well enough, or traveling in a country where I have no idea how anything works.

It's not a pleasant feeling, and "noob" is not meant as a compliment. But I recently realized there's something encouraging hidden in it: the more of a noob you are locally, the less of a noob you are globally.

Here's an example. If you stay in your home country, you'll feel less of a noob than if you move to Farawavia, where everything works differently. And yet you'll almost certainly know more if you make the move. Which means the feeling of being a noob is inversely correlated with actual ignorance.

If feeling like a noob is good for us, why do we dislike it? What evolutionary purpose could that dislike serve?

I think there are two sources of the feeling: being stupid, and doing something novel. Our dislike of feeling like a noob is our brain telling us "Come on, come on, figure this out." For most of history that was probably the right thing to be thinking. The life of hunter-gatherers was complex, but it didn't change as fast as life changes now. They weren't suddenly asked to figure out what to do about cryptocurrency. So it made sense to be biased toward competence at existing problems rather than the discovery of new ones. It made sense for humans to dislike the feeling of being a noob, just as, in a world where food was scarce, it made sense for them to dislike the feeling of being hungry.

But now too much food is more of a problem than too little, and our dislike of feeling hungry leads us astray. I think our dislike of feeling like a noob does the same thing.

Though the feeling is unpleasant, and though it's unflattering, and though people will sometimes laugh at you for it, the more you feel like a noob, the better off you are likely to be.
