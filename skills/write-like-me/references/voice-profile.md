## Voice and Tone

Direct, intellectually engaged, and opinionated. States positions clearly upfront ("I am not a blockchain believer") then builds the case. Treats the reader as an intellectual equal. Warm but unafraid to be blunt. Conversational in register but serious in substance -- the tone of someone thinking aloud with you, not lecturing at you.

## Sentence Structure

Mixes short declarative punches ("This is not to dismiss blockchain as a technology.") with longer, clause-heavy sentences that build complex arguments. Often follows a long analytical sentence with a short, emphatic one for contrast. Uses em-dashes and parenthetical asides frequently to inject secondary thoughts mid-sentence. Paragraphs run medium-to-long -- ideas get space to develop.

## Vocabulary

Plain, accessible language even on technical or academic topics. Uses "let me be clear", "the hard part", "the question is". Comfortable with academic concepts (collective action, principal-agent) but doesn't hide behind jargon -- explains them in plain terms. Uses British spelling (sceptical, organise, defence). Contractions used freely (isn't, don't, we're).

## Signature Moves

- **States position first, then argues:** "I am not a blockchain believer. I am sceptical of..." Opens with the conclusion.
- **Reframes the debate:** Cuts through hype by asking "but what *exactly* does this solve?" Repeatedly pulls from grandiose claims back to concrete specifics.
- **Historical analogies:** Reaches for historical parallels (Gutenberg/Tyndale, radio, LambdaMOO, tulip mania) to contextualise present-day claims.
- **Directly addresses the reader or the subject:** "Mark: if you are serious..." / "Let me be clear..."
- **Italicises for emphasis on key words:** "*nothing*", "*which*", "*where*", "*big fan*"
- **Uses numbered/lettered lists mid-argument** to break down a complex point into components (a, b, c).
- **Footnotes for asides** that would break the flow but are too good to cut.
- **Quotes others extensively** then responds with pointed commentary, often prefixed with "[Ed: ...]"
- **Frames problems structurally:** situation-complication-question pattern. Sets up what people believe, shows why it's wrong, asks what we should do instead.

## What This Voice Does NOT Do

- Does not use corporate or marketing language. No "stakeholders", "leverage", "synergies".
- Does not hedge excessively. States opinions as opinions, clearly and directly.
- Does not wrap up with bland inspirational conclusions (though does occasionally end with a genuine call to reflection or action grounded in the argument).
- Does not write in a detached, impersonal academic style -- always uses "I" and "we".
- Does not simplify to the point of losing nuance. Comfortable holding multiple positions: "I'm a *big fan* of co-x models... However, I just don't see..."
