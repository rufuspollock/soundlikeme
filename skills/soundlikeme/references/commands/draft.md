# draft

Write new text in the voice, from notes, bullets, a brief, or a conversation.

Load: the voice profile, [tells.md](../tells.md), [protections.md](../protections.md), [concision.md](../concision.md), [self-check.md](../self-check.md).

Harder than `polish`, because there is no draft holding the structure and the content in place. Everything comes from the profile and the notes, so both failure modes are live: you can drift generic, and you can drift into parody.

## Before writing

1. **Read the profile properly**, including the markers. You are going to be measured against them.
2. **Know the job.** What is this piece, who reads it, where does it appear, what should they think or do afterwards? Ask one question if genuinely unclear. Only one.
3. **Find the author's structural habit** for this register. Do they open with the position and then argue it? Build to it? Open with a story? Use their shape, not the default introduction-body-conclusion.
4. **Pick the signature moves** that fit this piece — three or four, not the whole list.

## While writing

- **Use only the user's material.** Their claims, examples, numbers, sources. Where the argument needs a fact you do not have, leave a marked gap: `[need: the 2019 figure]`. Never fabricate a statistic, a study, a quotation, or an anecdote to make prose feel concrete. This is the worst thing this command can do.
- **Write to the markers.** Sentence length and variance, paragraph length, contraction rate, spelling convention, em-dash and italic habits, footnote use.
- **Deploy signature moves at the profile's rate.** If the author reaches for a historical analogy once a piece, use one. Three is impersonation, and it reads worse than having used none.
- **Commit.** Most drafting failures are hedging failures. If the profile says the author states a position and defends it, state the position. Balanced-on-every-side is the default voice of the model, not of a person.

## After writing

Second pass over your own output, then [self-check.md](../self-check.md), all thirty items, paying attention to the Voice and Flatness blocks. Once.

Then a specific test: pick your three best sentences. Could they appear, unchanged, in a piece by any competent writer on this topic? If yes, the piece is generic and no amount of tell-removal will fix it.

## Output

The draft, then a short note listing any `[need: ...]` gaps and any place where you followed the profile against your own judgment — the second one is where the user's correction is most valuable.
