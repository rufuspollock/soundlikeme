# AI tells

Never load this without [protections.md](protections.md). A match here is evidence, not a
verdict.

Tells are tiered by how much a single instance tells you.

- **Hard** — fires on sight. One instance is enough.
- **Cluster** — fires only when several appear together, or one appears twice in a short piece.
- **Density** — fires only when the piece is saturated. Individually these are ordinary English.

## Hard: words

Fires on sight, in any register.

delve, tapestry, realm, multifaceted, ever-evolving, ever-changing, paradigm shift,
game-changer, transformative, testament to, beacon, symphony, intricate dance, treasure
trove, unlock the potential, supercharge, turbocharge, embark on a journey, navigate the
landscape, in the ever-evolving world of

## Hard: phrases

"It's important to note that", "It's worth noting that", "In today's fast-paced world",
"In today's digital age", "In the world of X", "In the age of X", "Let's dive in", "Let's
unpack this", "Let's break this down", "At the end of the day", "When it comes to X",
"the reality is", "the truth is", "here's the thing", "here's what nobody tells you",
"what most people get wrong", "the part everyone misses", "let that sink in",
"I'll be honest", "buckle up"

## Hard: chatbot residue

"Certainly!", "Of course!", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if", "Here is the", "As an
AI", knowledge-cutoff disclaimers, "I've drafted", section labels the user did not ask for.
These are pasted artifacts, not style. Always cut.

## Hard: structures

**Binary contrast.** "It's not X, it's Y." / "The question isn't X, it's Y." / "This isn't
just X — it's Y." State Y.
> "The question isn't the model, it's the eval." → "The eval matters more than the model."

**Negative listing.** "Not a tool. Not a framework. A philosophy." Say the last one.

**Colon reveal.** Noun phrase, colon, dramatic lowercase payoff. "The detail that makes it
work: a second agent grades it." Write it as a sentence.

**Self-answered rhetorical question.** "The result? Chaos." / "Why does this matter?
Because..." Fold into a statement.

**Faux-insight setup.** "This is the part most people skip." "Here's what they don't tell
you." Flatters the writer. Cut the setup; let the claim stand.

**Fake-profound kicker.** The final "deep" line that turns the argument into an aphorism.
Delete it — do not rewrite it into a better aphorism. End on the last concrete point.

**Summary-recap ending.** "In conclusion", "Ultimately", "Overall", or a closing paragraph
restating the piece. The reader was just there.

**Superficial participle analysis.** Trailing `-ing` clause pretending to explain
significance: "highlighting the company's commitment to innovation", "underscoring the
importance of", "reflecting a broader shift toward". Replace with the actual consequence or
cut.

**Importance puffery.** "stands as a testament to", "marks a pivotal moment", "plays a
vital role", "solidifies its position", "cannot be overstated". State the fact.

**Weasel attribution.** "Experts agree", "studies show", "industry reports suggest", "many
argue", "widely regarded as". Name the source or drop the claim. Never invent one — ask.

**Interpretive metadiscourse.** Lines that step outside the subject to tell the reader what
to notice: "This distinction matters", "That last part is more important than it sounds",
"As you can see", "In other words" when nothing was restated.

## Cluster

Individually defensible, collectively damning.

**Tricolon.** Three parallel items for rhythm. "Fast, cheap, and reliable." Two beats three.
One instance is fine. Three in a piece is a tell.

**Dramatic fragmentation.** "Speed. That's it. That's the whole trade-off." Stacked short
fragments manufacturing emphasis.

**Synonym cycling.** Rotating terms for variety when the plain word was right. "The agent
reviews the draft. The assistant scores the piece. The tool suggests fixes." One actor,
one name.

**Fake-strong verbs.** "serves as", "acts as a", "functions as", "represents a", "leverages",
"harnesses", "facilitates", "enables". Usually "is" or "has" was clearer and shorter.

**Inanimate agency.** Objects and abstractions performing human verbs. "The data suggests",
"the framework understands", "the complaint becomes a fix". Name the person.

**Vague declarative.** "The implications are significant." "The reasons are structural."
"The results speak for themselves." Says nothing. Name the implication.

**Robotic rhythm.** Three consecutive sentences of near-identical length, or every paragraph
built to the same shape.

**Meta-joiners.** "The rest of this piece will...", "In this section we'll explore...",
"Before we get to X, a word on Y."

**Uniform tone.** The most durable tell of all: register that never adapts to the audience,
the medium, or the paragraph's job. Every section equally warm, equally tidy, equally
balanced.

## Cluster: formatting

- Bold-label bullets where the bold text only restates what follows
- Emoji in headings
- Headers over two-sentence sections
- Bullets where two sentences of prose would read better
- Unicode arrows and decorative dividers
- Title Case Headings in a document that otherwise uses sentence case

## Density

Ordinary words. Only worth acting on when the piece is thick with them, and never when the
word is doing real work.

**Corporate verbs:** leverage, utilize, facilitate, foster, empower, streamline, robust,
elevate, unlock, drive, deliver, spearhead, curate, craft (as a verb for non-crafts)

**Inflation adjectives:** cutting-edge, innovative, seamless, comprehensive, holistic,
meticulous, nuanced, compelling, powerful, revolutionary, unprecedented, vital, crucial

**Empty adverbs:** just, really, very, quite, actually, simply, truly, literally, honestly,
fundamentally, importantly, crucially, inherently, inevitably, notably, arguably, quietly
(as a magic modifier: "quietly became the standard")

**Hedge stacks:** "may potentially", "could possibly", "might arguably", "it seems likely
that perhaps"

## Em dashes

Not banned. Over-used as a default rhythm crutch.

- Short copy: prefer none.
- Longer prose: fine where the dash genuinely beats a comma, colon, period, or parentheses.
- Remove clusters. Two in one paragraph is usually one too many.
- If the profile says the author uses them heavily, the profile wins. Some people really
  do write this way, and stripping them is a voice failure, not a slop fix.

## Maintenance

[Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing).

tell without a protection is how this file turns into a blunt instrument.
